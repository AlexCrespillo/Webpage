How drought travels
through rivers.

I am a PhD researcher at the Pyrenean Institute of Ecology (IPE‑CSIC), in the Laboratory of Climatology and Climate Services. I study how hydrological drought propagates through river networks: where it starts, how it moves downstream, and what controls the delay between one reach and the next.

About

I developed an event-based method that follows each drought from gauge to gauge along a river network, published in Journal of Hydrology in 2026 with open code. I am now using it to study how drought propagation has changed over recent decades in river basins around the world, and which physical processes control it.

I came to hydrology from geology and meteorology, and I am also studying mathematical engineering and high-performance computing, because most of what I do is processing long daily streamflow records from thousands of gauges.

Research

My thesis follows hydrological drought through the river network in four steps, from measuring it to predicting it.

  1. 01

    Tracking propagation, event by event

    Droughts are identified at each gauge from daily streamflow and matched between nested gauges along the network, which gives the delay, attenuation and spatial reach of every event.

    Published
  2. 02

    How propagation has changed

    Whether the way droughts propagate has changed over recent decades, in river networks across Europe and beyond, with tests designed to separate real change from artefacts of the record.

    In preparation
  3. 03

    What controls it

    The physical mechanisms behind the delays: network structure, catchment storage, groundwater, snow and river regulation, tested with hydrological modelling.

    Ongoing
  4. 04

    Predicting it

    Forecasting how a drought that has started upstream will reach downstream reaches, combining what the first three parts show with machine learning.

    Planned

I also contribute to the group's climate services for Spain on drought, dry spells and extreme rainfall, available at lcsc.csic.es.
